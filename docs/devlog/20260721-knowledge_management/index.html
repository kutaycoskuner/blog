My Approach to Academic Note-Taking and Knowledge Management

A Method for Synchronized Note-Taking

Since I started higher education, I have struggled with organizing my notes and accessing information systematically. Over time, these problems became centered around several key questions:

  • How should notes be structured?
  • What are the advantages and disadvantages of different note-taking methods?
  • Should I use digital or analog systems?
  • How can I create a system that remains useful in the long term?

For a long time, I relied mainly on notebooks because of their simplicity and speed. However, as the amount of information increased, several problems became apparent.

The Limitations of Analog Notes

  1. Searching and Accessing Information

As the number of notebooks grows, finding specific information becomes increasingly difficult. Unlike digital systems, where you can instantly search, filter, and categorize information, paper-based notes require manual organization and memory.

  1. Editing and Expanding Notes

Analog notes are also limited when it comes to revision. Usually, you write within a fixed physical space, which makes it difficult to restructure, expand, or reorganize existing information.

  1. Availability

Taking notes is not always possible. A notebook and a pen are physical objects, and you may not always have them with you when an idea, reference, or observation appears.

However, analog notes also have important advantages. They provide a natural environment for visual thinking. Drawings, diagrams, sketches, and other graphical elements can easily become part of your notes without technical limitations.

The Digital Alternative

Digital note-taking introduces its own advantages and disadvantages.

Advantage 1: Search, Organization, and Editing

When structured properly, digital notes provide powerful tools for searching, sorting, linking, and editing information. Large amounts of knowledge can be managed more efficiently compared to traditional notebooks.

Advantage 2: Accessibility

One of the strongest advantages of digital systems is accessibility. Notes can be created, edited, and accessed across different devices such as computers and smartphones.

For example, a quick thought captured on a phone can later become part of a larger research note without needing manual transcription.

Disadvantage 1: Dependency on Technology

Digital systems depend on hardware, electricity, and storage solutions. Unlike a notebook, which can simply be opened anywhere, digital systems require additional infrastructure.

Disadvantage 2: File Formats and Long-Term Preservatio

The format of your data becomes an important consideration. Proprietary binary formats can create dependency on specific applications or companies.

A digital system should therefore consider not only convenience but also long-term accessibility and ownership of information.

Of course, choosing between digital and analog notes does not have to be an absolute decision as they are not mutually exclusive. Different situations require different tools.

However, for long-term knowledge management, digital systems become increasingly valuable in my opinion because they allow structured organization, searchability, and flexible modification.

This leads to my current note-taking approach. The foundation of my system is based on plain text files, specifically Markdown. (In that manner I would like to remind and thanks once again to Aaron Swartz)

Design Principles Based on This Thought Process

When building this workflow, I was looking for a few important qualities:

  • Free and accessible: The system should not depend on paid subscriptions or locked features. Every essential component should be available without a paywall.
  • Ownership of data: My notes should remain my own files, stored in open formats rather than inside a proprietary platform.
  • Long-term sustainability: The system should remain usable years later, independent of a specific application or company.
  • Flexibility: Components should be replaceable. If one editor, synchronization tool, or workflow stops fitting my needs, I should be able to change it without losing my knowledge base.
  • Cross-platform availability: The same information should be accessible from different devices and operating systems.

These principles are the reason why this workflow is built around plain text, Markdown, Git, and peer-to-peer synchronization rather than a closed note-taking platform.

Why Plain Text Matters? Binary Formats vs. Plain Text

Understanding the difference between binary and plain text formats is important.

Binary formats store information in a way that is usually only meaningful to specific software. Examples include many proprietary document formats. They often provide advanced features but create dependency on a specific program.

Plain text files, on the other hand, are simple human-readable files. They can be opened, modified, and processed by almost any system.

Markdown provides a practical middle ground. It remains readable as plain text while allowing structured formatting and rendering into more visually appealing documents.

For mathematical notation, I use KaTeX syntax. Although Markdown is technically a markup language, it maintains readability while providing many of the advantages of richer formats such as HTML or XML.

Components of My System

1. Text Editor

The first component is a text editor.

I personally use Visual Studio Code, mostly because I am already familiar with it. However, the choice of editor is not important. Any capable text editor can work.

Some common alternatives include:

  • Vim
  • Emacs
  • Sublime Text
  • Kate
  • Nano

The important point is that the editor should work well with plain text files.

2. Storage and Version Control

The second component is version control.

I use Git to store and manage my notes.

Git allows me to:

  • keep a history of changes,
  • recover previous versions,
  • track modifications,
  • maintain backups across different locations.

For me, Git acts like a secure archive for my written knowledge. It provides confidence that my notes are not simply stored somewhere but are preserved with their entire history.

3. Synchronization Between Devices

The third component is synchronization.

Modern workflows often involve multiple devices. You may not always have access to your computer, but you usually have your smartphone nearby.

Taking quick notes on a phone is something I frequently do. However, manually transferring these notes later can become inefficient and can lead to forgotten information.

To solve this problem, I use Syncthing.

Syncthing creates direct peer-to-peer communication between my devices. Whenever the devices are available, changes are automatically synchronized between them.

The Result

With this approach, I have a system that provides such qualities:

  1. synchronized notes across multiple devices,
  2. version-controlled history,
  3. decentralized backups,
  4. flexible editing,
  5. easy searching and categorization,
  6. long-term accessibility.

The main idea is not that this is the only correct method of note-taking. Different people have different needs and workflows. However, I believe that combining the simplicity of plain text with modern tools such as Markdown, Git, and synchronization creates a powerful foundation for academic knowledge management.

I hope this approach is useful for others as well. If you have suggestions, improvements, or different approaches, I would be happy to hear them. Thank you for your time for reading this post!

obur taraftan dijital not kendi avantaj ve dezeavantaj setiyle beraber geliyor.

Binary ve plain text arasindaki farki anlamak neden plain text onemli?

  1. format: notlari markdown sisteminde yazip tutuyorum. nedeni ise basitligi, platformdan bagimsiz olarak herhangi bir programda acilabiliyor ve kolaylikl farkli sekilde renderlanabiliyor. Plain text file olusu uzun vadeli kaliciligi veya belli binary formatlara bagimliliktan kurtariyor. matematik noktasyonu konusunda ise katex markerlar kullaniyorum. Markdown temelde bir marking dili olmasina ragmen, html ve xml gibi okunabilirligi buyuk oranda dusurmeden, size guzel bir midground sagliyor.

  2. text editor: Plain text kontrolu icin, bir text editor. Ben vsc kullaniyorum genelde aliskanliktan geliyor siz istediginizi secebilirsiniz, old school abiler genelde vim, emacs kullaniyor. sublime text linuxta nano ve kate bazi alternatifleri.

Telefondan not almak benim siklikla yaptigim bir sey, fakat bu notlari sonradan akatarmak ciddi zaman kaybi veya unutmaya yol acabiliyor bunun icin Syncthing isimli bir programla, peer-to-peer cihazlarim arasinda iletisim kurarak aktif olduklari anda senkronizasyon sagliyoruz.

Elimizde farkli cihazlarimiz arasinda senkronize, geriye donuk versiyon konrollu ve birden fazla noktada yedegi bulunan, sort categorizasyon ve editing icin flexible bir sistem var.
