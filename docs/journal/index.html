Contradictions of Germany: Part II

Mean wealth is calculated by adding the total wealth of a population and dividing it by the number of people. It represents the average amount of wealth per person, but it can be strongly influenced by a small number of very wealthy individuals.

Median wealth is the wealth level of the person who is exactly in the middle of the population when everyone is ordered from the poorest to the richest. It represents the typical wealth of the population because extreme values have much less influence.

The difference between mean and median wealth is a good metric for measuring inequality of wealth distribution on the assumption that the data is correct. If the mean is much higher than the median, it suggests that wealth is concentrated among a small group of people, while a large part of the population owns significantly less. Therefore, median wealth often gives a better picture of how wealthy the average person actually is.

The graph is therefore not simply showing that Germany as a country is poor; it is showing that wealth is distributed very unevenly. A relatively small group owns a large share of the total wealth, while many people have comparatively low personal assets. Furthermore, if some groups with lower accumulated wealth are not represented in the statistics, such as parts of the foreign workforce living in Germany (as they are not citizen might not appear on the data), the real median wealth of the entire population could be even much more lower.

Contradictions of Germany: In a weakening economy, Germany is granting special status to nightclubs to keep them from going under. The main result of this, is tax reductions.

This is the entertainment sector. It is non-essential and discretionary in nature. In that case, shouldn’t it be subject to higher taxation compared to essential sectors?

As a result, the primary financial benefit tends to stay with business owners rather than workers.

Germany increasingly feels like a country where people who simply want to work honestly and live with dignity end up being penalized. But from the outside, and without looking too deeply, it often appears like a very “cool” country — the typical PR image they’ve always managed to project.

Something to appreciate is that we should not be under the illusion that the rights we have today have always existed. At various points in history, people with imagination and vision created artificial systems to overcome the cruelties of existence. That is one part of the story.

However, man-made systems do not automatically become better than what exists in nature. In fact, they can sometimes become even more cruel or worse.

At the same time, many people have fought for justice. What we have today is not the result of something that has always been this way, but the result of sacrifice, struggle, and resistance.

Being aware of this can cultivate gratitude toward those who came before us and fought for these rights. It also reminds us that if we forget this history or fail to do our part in preserving it, we risk losing what has been gained.
