That is why I believe every person should develop their own system of reasoning instead of acting based solely on the thoughts of others. I value and proud my validated logic through cost of experience, and though I keep inquiring them. Since childhood I have used certain logical shortcuts whose validity I have confirmed through different situations. I decided to put them into writing. I will share them as they come to mind as social-lemmas.

Social Lemma (The Fallibility of Consistent Truth): The fact that nine statements made by a person have been correct does not imply that the tenth will also be correct. Therefore, critical thinking should never be abandoned, and each statement should be evaluated independently of its source.

I’ve often witnessed that people I know to be generally correct and whose insights I respect can start to talk nonsense at certain points. 2026-05-08 opinion

There is something deeply wrong with formal mathematics education, or at least the form I have encountered.

The main problem is that we are given the most abstract, filtered results of mathematical analysis, packed into concise formal sentences. These statements just float in a sea of meaninglessness without context. You are expected, if you are patient and lucky enough, to slowly piece together why and how they became necessary in the first place. The way mathematics is often taught makes it seem as if mathematicians simply invented these results out of nowhere. In reality, they encountered problems and developed solutions. Presenting only the final form removes the reasoning behind it, which is not only against scientific thinking but also against logic itself.

Another thing is the fetishization of formalization. Desire to compress everything into compact mathematical statements. Graphs, visual models, and other forms of representation that would actually aid comprehension and intuition are largely absent. Seeing coordinate systems in analysis or linear algebra only a few times per semester is, in my opinion, should not be the thing in education in mathematics.

Georg Cantor’s diagonal argument just doesn’t convince me, or maybe I don’t understand it. Or at least, I haven’t come across anyone who explains the proof in a causal way beyond just parroting it. The fact that Cantor himself claimed the proof was revealed to him by God doesn’t really help clear up the doubts in my mind either.

2026-05-01 opinion

Passing judgment on matters we do not fully understand puts us in an unjustifiable position. Saying ‘I didn’t know’ or appealing to ignorance should not be accepted as an excuse in such cases.

Before reaching a judgment, one should make sure to have carefully analyzed the context and the situation as a whole, using the means available to us and putting in the necessary effort. Even if no one will hold us accountable, this matters if one value of being true.

Forming a judgment is actually very easy, and people often admire those who speak decisively because of the confidence they project. But what should be focused on is not the judgment itself, but how that judgment was reached.
